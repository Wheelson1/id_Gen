Why a copy doesn't work

Four protections, each one closing a different way people cheat today.

  1. Signed by the university

    Each card's QR code carries a digital signature (Ed25519) made with a key only the university's server holds. It covers the card number, matric number, name and expiry, so changing any of them breaks the signature.

  2. A live pass that changes every minute

    For gates and exams, students show a pass on their phone that is replaced every 60 seconds and works once. A screenshot or a forwarded picture is refused as a replay.

  3. Checked against the register, every time

    A valid signature isn't enough: each scan also checks whether the card has been withdrawn, reported lost, or has expired.

  4. Protected staff accounts and a full record

    Registry staff sign in with a password and a code from an authenticator app. Every card issued, withdrawn or scanned is logged with who did it and when. Students can't look up other people's details: the name stays hidden until the date of birth matches, and repeated guessing locks the lookup.

What the university controls

The card design

Registry admins design the card themselves in the Card Design Lab: layout, sizes, colours, wording and crest. The system won't save a design whose QR code would be too small to scan.

Who holds a valid card

A card reported lost or a student withdrawn is refused at every scanner from that moment. Every change needs a reason and is recorded.

The data and the money

Student records stay in the university's own database. Card fees are paid through Paystack and can be split directly into the university's account.

How we would roll it out

  1. Set up

    Import the admission list, set the card fee and payment account, and give registry staff their protected accounts.

  2. Pilot with one faculty

    Students in one faculty get their cards online; security at selected gates and exam halls start scanning.

  3. Campus-wide

    Open to every student, print physical cards in batches for those who want one, and train all gate and exam staff.
